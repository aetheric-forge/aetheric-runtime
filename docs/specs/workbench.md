# Constitutional Article — Workbench

## Article IX — Workbench

### §1. Definition

A **Workbench** is an Institution in which materials, knowledge, instructions, and other resources are assembled, examined, transformed, and prepared into works.

The Workbench provides a governed environment for institutional making, experimentation, and production.

A work present within the Workbench is not, by that presence alone, an authoritative fact, a constitutional record, or a curated work of knowledge.

---

### §2. Purpose

Every Campus **SHALL** maintain a Workbench.

The purpose of the Workbench is to enable useful work to be undertaken safely, deliberately, and accountably within the institutional hierarchy.

The Workbench exists to support construction, transformation, repair, experimentation, validation, and other productive activity consistent with institutional purpose and policy.

---

### §3. Work

**Work** is a governed activity undertaken to produce, modify, examine, or evaluate one or more works.

Work **MAY** be performed by Persons, services, agents, tools, or other workers acting within the authority of the Workbench.

Every act of work **SHALL** remain attributable to its responsible authority and institutional context.

The Workbench **SHALL NOT** represent incomplete, failed, or experimental work as complete without sufficient basis.

---

### §4. Works and Materials

A **work** is an object, composition, result, or other product upon which work is performed or which work produces.

**Materials** are resources supplied to, consumed by, incorporated into, or transformed through work.

Works and materials **MAY** include physical, informational, computational, or conceptual forms.

The Workbench **SHALL** preserve the distinction between source materials, intermediate works, and completed results where that distinction is material to their interpretation or use.

---

### §5. Staging

The Workbench **MAY** maintain one or more stages in which works and materials are held during active or anticipated work.

Staging is provisional custody for work. It does not, by itself, constitute publication, registration, archival preservation, or inclusion in a Library.

Every staged object **SHALL** be addressable within its stage and **SHOULD** retain sufficient metadata to support its identification, handling, and intended disposition.

The Workbench **MAY** impose limits upon the duration, volume, visibility, or use of staged objects according to institutional purpose, policy, and available resources.

---

### §6. Artificer

The **Artificer** is the Authority responsible for the conduct and stewardship of work within the Workbench.

The Artificer coordinates workers, governs access to staged works and materials, and safeguards the integrity of work in progress.

The Artificer **MAY** delegate work to authorized workers, but remains accountable for the exercise of delegated authority.

The Artificer **SHALL NOT** claim authority over a work, resource, or worker beyond that granted by the Institution.

---

### §7. Workers and Tools

A **worker** is a Person, service, agent, device, process, or other participant authorized to perform work within the Workbench.

Workers act under the authority of the Artificer and within the policies governing the work.

Tools extend the means by which work may be performed; possession or operation of a tool does not confer authority to determine the purpose, legitimacy, or disposition of the work.

The use of autonomous or external workers **SHALL NOT** diminish institutional accountability for work undertaken through the Workbench.

---

### §8. Coordination and Integrity

The Workbench **SHALL** provide means sufficient to prevent incompatible operations from silently corrupting or invalidating work.

Works **MAY** be reserved, locked, isolated, copied, or otherwise coordinated while operations are in progress.

Coordination mechanisms establish operational control only. They do not establish ownership, authorship, constitutional authority, or exclusive legitimacy.

Failure, cancellation, interruption, or contention **SHOULD** leave the state and disposition of affected work discoverable to those responsible for it.

---

### §9. Retention and Disposition

Staged works and materials are not presumed to be permanent.

The Workbench **MAY** expire, remove, replace, or release staged objects according to declared policy and the requirements of active work.

A staged object required for active work **MAY** be protected from removal for the duration of that requirement.

Protection within staging does not create a duty of permanent preservation. Works requiring institutional continuity **SHALL** be transferred to the appropriate Archive, Library, register, or other Institution according to their constitutional meaning.

---

### §10. Provenance and Reproducibility

The Workbench **SHOULD** preserve sufficient provenance to identify, where applicable:

- The work undertaken
- Source materials
- Responsible authority
- Participating workers
- Tools and methods
- Time and context
- Result and disposition

The degree of provenance required **MAY** vary according to the consequence, reversibility, and institutional significance of the work.

Where reproducibility is required by purpose or policy, the Workbench **SHALL** preserve sufficient information to reproduce or meaningfully evaluate the work.

---

### §11. Safety and Constraint

Work **SHALL** be conducted according to applicable law, policy, authority, and institutional purpose.

The Workbench **MAY** isolate work, restrict tools or materials, require review, or suspend activity where necessary to protect Persons, Institutions, resources, knowledge, or the integrity of other work.

Experimentation does not exempt work from constitutional accountability.

Restrictions upon work **SHOULD** be proportionate to the risks and obligations they address.

---

### §12. Constitutional Relationship

The Workbench produces and transforms works; it does not determine their constitutional meaning merely by producing them.

The Registrar establishes authoritative institutional facts.

The Archive preserves historical and constitutional records.

The Library curates knowledge for discovery, interpretation, teaching, and reuse.

Post communicates works and instructions between participants and institutional scopes.

A work **MAY** pass from the Workbench to one or more of these Institutions, but each transition **SHALL** occur according to the authority, policy, and provenance appropriate to the receiving Institution.

No act of staging, transformation, validation, completion, or release by the Workbench **SHALL**, by itself, register, archive, publish, endorse, or curate a work.

---

### §13. Implementation Independence

The constitutional definition of a Workbench is independent of any implementation technology, storage medium, execution environment, orchestration system, tool, or deployment model.

Stages, workers, tools, and coordination mechanisms **MAY** be replaced without changing the constitutional identity or purpose of the Workbench.

Implementations **SHALL** preserve the constitutional properties defined herein.

---

### §14. Invariants

Every Workbench **SHALL**:

- Enable governed work in service of institutional purpose.
- Operate through an accountable Artificer.
- Preserve the distinction between source materials, intermediate works, and completed results where material.
- Distinguish provisional staging from publication, registration, archival preservation, and curation.
- Coordinate incompatible operations sufficiently to protect the integrity of work.
- Preserve accountability for delegated and autonomous work.
- Apply lawful and proportionate constraints to work.
- Remain independent of implementation technology.

No Workbench **SHALL** present provisional custody, operational control, or successful production as constitutional authority over a work.
