# Constitutional Article — Campus

## Article VI — Campus

### §1. Definition

A **Campus** is the unique root Institution of an institutional hierarchy.

Every institutional hierarchy **SHALL** contain exactly one Campus, from which all other Institutions derive their structural context.

The Campus establishes the identity, continuity, and constitutional boundary of the hierarchy.

---

### §2. Purpose

The Campus provides the common institutional context within which all subordinate Institutions exist.

It serves as the constitutional anchor for governance, identity, membership, archives, and organizational relationships.

---

### §3. Root Authority

The Campus possesses no parent Institution.

It represents the highest level of structural authority within its own hierarchy.

No Institution may exist outside the Campus that defines its context.

---

### §4. Recursive Hierarchy

Every Institution other than the Campus **SHALL** exist as a descendant of exactly one parent Institution.

This recursive relationship forms a rooted, directed, acyclic hierarchy.

The Campus constitutes the root of this hierarchy.

---

### §5. Containment

Institutional containment represents constitutional ownership and administrative responsibility.

Containment **SHALL NOT** be inferred from communication, collaboration, governance, or operational interaction.

Only explicit parent-child relationships define structural containment.

---

### §6. Institutional Identity

The Campus defines the shared institutional identity of all descendant Institutions.

Individual Institutions **MAY** possess their own identities, missions, and governance, but these **SHALL** exist within the constitutional context established by the Campus.

---

### §7. Campus Boundary

The Campus establishes the constitutional boundary separating the institutional hierarchy from external organizations.

Relationships crossing this boundary **SHALL** be explicitly represented through constitutional relationships rather than structural containment.

---

### §8. Relationships

The Campus **MAY** maintain constitutional, operational, academic, financial, or collaborative relationships with external entities.

Such relationships **SHALL NOT** modify the structural hierarchy.

---

### §9. Campus Context

The Campus defines the common context in which:

- Institutions exist.
- Governance operates.
- Members participate.
- Archives preserve institutional memory.
- Identity is established.
- Constitutional authority is exercised.

---

### §10. Institutional Capabilities

Every Campus **SHALL** maintain:

- An Archive
- A Library
- A Post Office
- A Registrar

These Institutions serve the Campus and **MAY** serve descendant Institutions through the institutional hierarchy.

The requirement that a Campus maintain these capabilities **SHALL NOT** make them intrinsic properties of every Institution.

---

### §11. Continuity

The existence of subordinate Institutions depends upon the constitutional continuity of the Campus.

Changes to the Campus **SHALL** preserve the continuity and integrity of the institutional hierarchy.

---

### §12. Technology Independence

The constitutional definition of a Campus is independent of any implementation technology, software architecture, database model, or deployment environment.

Implementations **SHALL** preserve the constitutional properties defined herein.

---

### §13. Invariants

Every Campus **SHALL** preserve the following invariants:

- Every institutional hierarchy contains exactly one Campus.
- The Campus has no parent Institution.
- Every other Institution is a descendant of the Campus.
- Institutional containment forms a rooted, acyclic hierarchy.
- Structural containment **SHALL NOT** be inferred from communication or collaboration.
- The Campus defines the constitutional context of the hierarchy.
- The Campus maintains an Archive, Library, Post Office, and Registrar.
