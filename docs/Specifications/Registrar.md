# Constitutional Article — Registrar

## Article IV — Registrar

### §1. Definition

A **Registrar** is the constitutional authority by which an Institution records, recognizes, and attests institutional facts.

The Registrar establishes the authoritative standing of identities, roles, relationships, credentials, appointments, authorities, and other facts within the Institution.

The Registrar does not create identity.

---

### §2. Purpose

Every Institution **SHALL** maintain a Registrar.

The purpose of the Registrar is to provide an authoritative and discoverable account of the facts recognized by the Institution.

The Registrar exists to enable lawful recognition, reliable administration, accountability, and institutional continuity.

---

### §3. Authority

The Registrar acts only under authority granted by the constitution, governance, or lawful delegation of the Institution.

Registration records the exercise or recognition of authority; it does not create authority where none exists.

The Registrar **SHALL NOT** recognize a claim as authoritative without a sufficient constitutional basis.

---

### §4. Recognition

A registered fact is a fact the Institution formally recognizes for institutional purposes.

Recognition **MAY** concern identity, standing, membership, role, appointment, delegation, qualification, relationship, ownership, or other matters within the lawful competence of the Institution.

Absence from a register does not, by itself, establish nonexistence.

---

### §5. Identity

Identity exists independently of registration.

The Registrar **MAY** associate an identity with institutional facts, but **SHALL NOT** define, possess, or silently replace that identity.

Registration establishes what the Institution recognizes about an identity, not whether that identity exists.

---

### §6. Attestation

The Registrar **MAY** attest that a registered fact is recognized by the Institution.

Every attestation **SHALL** identify the fact attested, the authority under which it is recognized, and the relevant institutional context.

An attestation conveys institutional recognition only within the scope of the Registrar's authority.

---

### §7. Provenance

Every registered fact **SHALL** retain sufficient provenance to establish:

- Subject
- Source
- Authority
- Time
- Context
- Constitutional Basis

The provenance of a registration is part of the registration itself.

---

### §8. Amendment and Revocation

A registered fact **MAY** be amended, superseded, suspended, expired, or revoked according to the constitution and policies of the Institution.

Such action **SHALL** produce a new authoritative state and **SHALL NOT** silently alter the historical record.

Revocation ends or limits present recognition; it does not erase the fact that recognition previously existed.

---

### §9. Accessibility

Registered facts **SHOULD** remain discoverable to those entitled to rely upon them.

Accessibility does not imply unrestricted visibility.

Institutions **MAY** restrict access according to law, policy, privacy, security, or constitutional authority while preserving the integrity and attestability of the register.

---

### §10. Impartiality

The Registrar **SHALL** apply the same constitutional standards to equivalent claims.

Administrative custody does not confer discretion to alter institutional truth, invent authority, or withhold recognition contrary to the constitution.

The Registrar serves the Institution and remains accountable to its governance.

---

### §11. Constitutional Relationship

The Registrar serves every constitutional entity whose standing or relationships require institutional recognition.

Institutions, Communities, Organizations, Persons, Roles, Policies, Resources, and Governance **MAY** derive authoritative standing through registration.

The Archive preserves the historical record of registrations and their evolution; the Registrar establishes their authoritative institutional state.

---

### §12. Invariants

Every Registrar **SHALL**:

- Act under lawful institutional authority.
- Distinguish identity from institutional recognition.
- Preserve the provenance of registered facts.
- Support authoritative attestation.
- Record amendment, succession, and revocation without silent replacement.
- Apply constitutional standards consistently.
- Remain independent of implementation technology.

No Registrar **SHALL** create authority merely by recording it or erase the historical existence of an authoritative institutional fact.
