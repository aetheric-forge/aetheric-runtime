# Constitutional Article — Archive

## Article III — Archive

### §1. Definition

An **Archive** is the constitutional record of an Institution's existence.

It preserves the history, evolution, decisions, and constitutional state of an Institution across time.

The Archive exists to ensure continuity of identity, accountability, and institutional memory.

---

### §2. Purpose

Every Institution **SHALL** maintain an Archive.

The purpose of the Archive is to preserve an accurate and discoverable record of constitutional and operational history.

The Archive exists to enable understanding, accountability, continuity, and lawful succession.

---

### §3. Continuity

An Institution's Archive persists for the lifetime of the Institution.

The dissolution, restructuring, or amendment of an Institution **SHALL NOT** invalidate or erase its historical record.

---

### §4. Integrity

Archived information **SHALL** preserve its authenticity.

Historical records **SHALL NOT** be modified to alter historical truth.

Corrections, annotations, or superseding records **MAY** be appended, but historical records remain part of the constitutional record.

---

### §5. Provenance

Every archived record **SHALL** retain sufficient provenance to establish:

- Origin
- Authorship
- Authority
- Time
- Context
- Constitutional Basis

The provenance of a record is part of the record itself.

---

### §6. Evolution

Constitutional amendments, policy revisions, governance changes, organizational restructuring, and significant institutional events **SHALL** become part of the Archive.

The Archive records both the current constitutional state and the path by which that state came into existence.

---

### §7. Accessibility

Archived information **SHOULD** remain discoverable according to the governance and policies of the Institution.

Accessibility does not imply unrestricted visibility.

Institutions **MAY** restrict access while preserving the existence and integrity of archived records.

---

### §8. Immutability

Archived records are immutable.

When information changes, the Institution records a new constitutional state rather than replacing an existing one.

The Archive preserves historical truth through accumulation rather than substitution.

---

### §9. Stewardship

The Archive is stewarded on behalf of the Institution.

Custodians safeguard its integrity but do not possess authority to redefine history.

Administrative control does not imply constitutional ownership.

---

### §10. Constitutional Relationship

The Archive serves every constitutional entity.

Institutions, Communities, Organizations, Persons, Roles, Policies, Resources, and Governance derive historical continuity through their archived constitutional record.

---

### §11. Invariants

Every Archive **SHALL**:

- Preserve institutional history.
- Maintain historical integrity.
- Record provenance.
- Support constitutional continuity.
- Preserve superseded constitutional states.
- Permit annotation without historical replacement.
- Remain independent of implementation technology.

No Archive **SHALL** permit the silent destruction or alteration of constitutional history.
